Everything you get

Each feature below shows the exact text limitcast prints. The numbers in the samples are an example; yours come from your own readings and transcripts.

Your limits in the status line Free

Every Claude Code prompt shows how much of each window you've used, when it runs out if you keep this pace, and today's even share of the week. Your own status line stays exactly as it was; limitcast adds to it.

Pro adds a pointer to your other Claude account when this one is nearly out, and a /compact nudge once a session re-sends more than ~150k tokens per call.

Subagent guard Pro Free preview

A burst of subagents is the most common way to lose the end of a window. When a window is on pace to run out within 30 minutes, the guard stops new subagents in Claude Code and Codex. While a Claude window is merely tight, new subagents run on Sonnet instead of your main model. Once you're past 100% it steps aside, so paid extra usage keeps working.

Free installs run it as a preview: it never acts, and results shows what it would have stopped.

# a new subagent, 21 minutes before the window runs out:
limitcast: Claude 5h limit is 93% used and on pace to run out in
~21 min, before it resets. Finish with the agents already running,
or set LIMITCAST_ALLOW=1 to spawn anyway.

# a new subagent while the window is tight:
limitcast: Claude 5h limit is 88% used; this subagent runs on
Sonnet. Set LIMITCAST_ALLOW=1 to keep the main model.

Subagent receipts and budgets Pro New in 0.11

Each time a subagent finishes, you see what it cost against your 5-hour window, priced from your own readings (the model never sees it). Set a budget and the guard holds new subagents once they've used that share of the current window, until it resets. 

limitcast: that Explore subagent used ≈2.1% of your 5-hour window
(34 calls, 180k new tokens).

$ limitcast budget subagents 15
Subagent budget: 15% of each 5-hour window. Used this window so far: ≈6.4%.
Once it's spent, the guard holds new subagents until the window resets.

Cold-cache warning Pro New in 0.11

Come back to a big session after a long break and the prompt cache has expired: your next message re-sends the whole context as new tokens. limitcast holds that first message once and tells you what it will cost, so you can /compact first. Send it again and it goes through. The cache lifetime is 60 minutes by default (LIMITCAST_CACHE_MINUTES).

Free installs record what it would have held; results shows it.

limitcast: this session sat idle for 75 minutes, so its prompt cache has
expired. Your message re-sends 300k tokens of context: ≈1.8% of your
5-hour window (≈0.03% if the cache were still warm). Send it again to go
ahead, or run /compact first to shrink it.

Notifications: desktop, phone or Slack Pro

One notification when a Claude window turns NEAR, AT_RISK or HIT, and one when it resets after a HIT, so you can step away and come back at the right time. On macOS they come from limitcast's own notifier app, listed under its name in System Settings. Linux uses notify-send.

New in 0.11: the same messages on your phone through ntfy (limitcast notify ntfy <topic>) or in a Slack or Discord channel (limitcast notify webhook <url>). Opt-in; only the notification text is sent, to the service you chose.

Trim advisor, with apply and undo Pro

Measures what your latest session loaded before you typed (hook output, MCP servers, tool names, skills, agents, instruction files), names the ones you haven't used, and what each costs. One command hides the skills from the model and turns the servers off for that project; another puts everything back. If Claude Code overwrites the settings, the cuts come back at the next session.

Free shows the totals: your starting context and how much of it you never use.

Takes effect in your next Claude Code session. Undo: `limitcast trim --undo`

Limit-change alert Pro New in 0.11

Every day limitcast re-measures how many tokens move your 5-hour meter by 1%, from your own readings. When that shifts by 15% or more against the weeks before, you get a notification, so you know when the limit itself changed rather than your habits.

FAQ

Does it send my code or prompts anywhere?

No. limitcast reads the limit readings Claude Code passes to your status line and the logs Claude Code and Codex already keep on your disk, and none of that leaves your machine. The only network calls are for a Pro license key: once to activate, a weekly re-check, and when you deactivate. They send your license key and computer name to Polar, our payment provider. The status line and hooks never wait on the network, and Pro keeps working offline for 30 days after the last check. If you turn on phone or webhook notifications (Pro, off by default), each notification's text goes to the service you chose (ntfy.sh or your webhook), and nothing else does.

Does it use my Claude login?

No. It never touches your subscription credentials, so it can't put your account at risk.

I use extra usage past my limit. Will the guard block me?

No. Once a window is used up, limitcast steps aside and leaves it to Claude Code. The guard only acts while you're heading toward a limit.

Does it work with Codex and Grok?

Codex: yes, forecasts, the subagent guard and the burn report, from the logs Codex already writes. Grok: the burn report covers your Grok tokens, but Grok keeps no limit readings on disk, so there's no Grok runout forecast and the guard never blocks Grok.

What if I have two Claude accounts?

Sessions of the same account share one history (matched by their limit reset times and usage that only rises), so even a short session gets a forecast, and two accounts used in parallel never mix.

Will one heavy hour make it say I'll run out of my weekly limit?

No. The 5-hour window is forecast from the last hour's pace. The weekly window uses your average pace over the last full day, idle time included, so a burst on Monday doesn't predict a runout by Wednesday. The exception is a burst that would use up the week within the next hour: that still triggers the warning and the subagent guard.

How does the free trial work?

Pick yearly or monthly and you get Pro for 14 days at no charge: your license key is ready as soon as you check out, in Polar's customer portal (linked from your confirmation email). Your card is charged when the trial ends. Cancel before then, in the same portal, and you pay nothing and the key stops working.

Can I get a refund?

Yes, within 14 days of any charge, no questions asked. Email support@limitcast.dev from the address you bought with and we'll refund it and cancel the subscription.
