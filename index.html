Everything you get

Each feature below shows the exact text limitcast prints. The numbers in the samples are an example; yours come from your own readings and transcripts.

Your limits in the status line Free

Every Claude Code prompt shows how much of each window you've used, when it runs out if you keep this pace, and today's even share of the week. Your own status line stays exactly as it was; limitcast adds to it.

Pro adds a pointer to your other Claude account when this one is nearly out, and a /compact nudge once a session re-sends more than ~150k tokens per call. 

5h 71% ⚠ out 16:40 · 7d 41% · today 6% left

# Pro, when you need it:
5h 96% ⚠ near limit · 7d 58% · today 2% over
limitcast: ⇄ other account 5h 12% · 7d 30% · ctx 162k · /compact

Runout forecast Free

Burn rate and the clock time each 5-hour and weekly window runs out, for Claude Code and Codex, and for Grok once you set a dollar cap. The weekly window is paced over the last full day, nights included, so one busy hour doesn't cry wolf. NEAR at 95%, even before there's a rate.

New in 0.14: when a window is on pace to run out, status ends with one line that names the one that runs out first and where else there is room. 

$ limitcast status
window     used  burn      runs out  resets     today     status
claude 5h  71%   +18.2%/h  16:40     17:00      -         AT_RISK
claude 7d  41%   +0.6%/h   -         Sun 03:00  6% left   OK
codex 5h   22%   +4.1%/h   -         18:12      -         OK

You'll hit Claude 5h first (16:40). Codex 5h has 78% left.

Subagent guard Pro Free preview

A burst of subagents is the most common way to lose the end of a window. When a window is on pace to run out within 30 minutes, the guard stops new subagents in Claude Code and Codex (and in Grok once you set a dollar cap). While a Claude window is merely tight, new subagents run on Sonnet instead of your main model. Once you're past 100% it steps aside, so paid extra usage keeps working.

Free installs run it as a preview: it never acts, and results shows what it would have stopped.

# a new subagent, 21 minutes before the window runs out:
limitcast: Claude 5h limit is 93% used and on pace to run out in
~21 min, before it resets. Finish with the agents already running,
or set LIMITCAST_ALLOW=1 to spawn anyway.

# a new subagent while the window is tight:
limitcast: Claude 5h limit is 88% used; this subagent runs on
Sonnet. Set LIMITCAST_ALLOW=1 to keep the main model.

Your week, paced by the day Pro Free preview New in 0.14

The today figure in your status line is your share of what is left of the week, split evenly over the days until it resets. Now it acts: with notifications on, you get one message a day when you have used 80% of today's share. Turn on limitcast budget today on and the guard also holds new subagents once today's share is used up, until local midnight.

The hold is off by default. A window already at 100% is left to the tool, and LIMITCAST_ALLOW=1 overrides it. Free installs record what Pro would have held, and results shows it. 

# once a day, at 80% of today's share:
limitcast: Claude 7d: 80% of today's share used, 1% left today.

# a new subagent, with the hold on:
limitcast: today's share of your Claude 7d limit is used up (6% of
5%). New subagents are held until midnight. Turn this off:
`limitcast budget today off`, or set LIMITCAST_ALLOW=1 to
spawn anyway.

A model for each kind of subagent Pro New in 0.13

Most subagents just read and search, and they inherit your main model. Pin a kind to a cheaper one and every new subagent of that kind runs on it, for example Explore on Haiku. A spawn that names its own model is left alone, and while a window is tight a kind pinned to Opus still moves to Sonnet. results counts the subagents it moved.

Needs limitcast install --guard. Free installs record what it would have done. Whether a cheaper model costs less of your limit per token isn't something limitcast can tell, so check report --why after a week. 

$ limitcast subagent-model Explore haiku
Explore: haiku

Types you ran in the last 30 days: general-purpose (86), Explore (39), fork (39)
New subagents of these types run on that model unless the spawn names one itself.

# the next Explore subagent:
limitcast: Explore subagents run on haiku, the model you set for them.

Subagent receipts and budgets Pro New in 0.11

Each time a subagent finishes, you see what it cost against your 5-hour window, priced from your own readings (the model never sees it). Set a budget and the guard holds new subagents once they've used that share of the current window, until it resets.

New in 0.12: before a new subagent starts, you see what that kind has cost you so far. It appears once you have 3 priced runs, and only when a typical run is over 1% of a window or the window is near its limit. It never blocks. 

limitcast: that Explore subagent used ≈2.1% of your 5-hour window
(34 calls, 180k new tokens).

limitcast: Explore subagents have cost you a median ≈1.2% (max ≈3.6%) of
a 5-hour window over 7 runs; this window is 38% used.

Once it's spent, the guard holds new subagents until the window resets.

Cold-cache warning Pro New in 0.11

Come back to a big session after a long break and the prompt cache has expired: your next message re-sends the whole context as new tokens. limitcast holds that first message once and tells you what it will cost, so you can /compact first. Send it again and it goes through. The cache lifetime is 60 minutes by default (LIMITCAST_CACHE_MINUTES).

Free installs record what it would have held; results shows it.

limitcast: this session sat idle for 75 minutes, so its prompt cache has
expired. Your message re-sends 300k tokens of context: ≈1.8% of your
5-hour window (≈0.03% if the cache were still warm). Send it again to go
ahead, or run /compact first to shrink it.

Notifications: desktop, phone or Slack Pro

One notification when a Claude window turns NEAR, AT_RISK or HIT, and one when it resets after a HIT, so you can step away and come back at the right time. On macOS they come from limitcast's own notifier app, listed under its name in System Settings. Linux uses notify-send.

New in 0.11: the same messages on your phone through ntfy (limitcast notify ntfy <topic>) or in a Slack or Discord channel (limitcast notify webhook <url>). Opt-in; only the notification text is sent, to the service you chose.

Trim advisor, with apply and undo Pro

Measures what your latest session loaded before you typed (hook output, MCP servers, tool names, skills, agents, instruction files), names the ones you haven't used, and what each costs. One command hides the skills from the model and turns the servers off for that project; another puts everything back. If Claude Code overwrites the settings, the cuts come back at the next session. Codex has trim --codex, and new in 0.12 Grok has trim --grok: its system prompt and the MCP servers in ~/.grok/config.toml you haven't used.

New in 0.14: limitcast trim keep ego-browser puts a server or skill on a keep-list that no --apply ever turns off or hides, for Claude Code, Codex and Grok. It is free to set, and trim shows what it would keep.

Free shows the totals: your starting context and how much of it you never use.

$ limitcast trim --apply
✓ Hid 66 skills from the model (`/name` still works): dataviz, block-kit, …
✓ Turned off 9 MCP servers for ~/work/api: postman, context7, …
Takes effect in your next Claude Code session. Undo: `limitcast trim --undo`

$ limitcast trim --codex --apply
✓ Turned off 3 Codex MCP servers (enabled = false in ~/.codex/config.toml):
  firecrawl, icm, node_repl

$ limitcast trim keep ego-browser
Kept by `trim --apply`: ego-browser

Grok Free Pro budget New in 0.12

limitcast install wraps Grok's status line: session model, context % and cost on one line, with the limitcast forecast under it. --guard adds a guard for spawn_subagent, and trim --grok measures what Grok loads before you type.

Grok stores what you spent, not a percentage, so the status line shows your 5-hour and 7-day spend until you set a dollar cap. With a cap (Pro), limitcast forecasts that window and the guard blocks new Grok subagents when it is on pace to run out. 

$ limitcast install --guard
$ limitcast budget grok 20
$ limitcast trim --grok

Does your plan fit? Pro New in 0.13

limitcast keeps the peak of every 5-hour and weekly window you have. Tell it your plan and it says whether you reached your limits often enough to look at the next one, or used so little that a smaller one would have fit, with the monthly price difference. It also shows what your tokens would cost at API list prices, so you can see what your plan covers.

It needs a week or two of history before it recommends anything, and it cannot see extra-usage spend. Anthropic states the 5-hour multiplier for Max (5x or 20x Pro); the advisor assumes weekly limits scale the same way. Example output below; your numbers will differ.

Large tool-output notice Pro New in 0.12

A tool result enters your context once as new tokens and is then re-read on every later call, so one very large result is worth knowing about. When a single result is about 20k tokens or more, limitcast tells you (never the model), once per tool per session, with what to ask for next time. Turn it on with limitcast install --alerts; LIMITCAST_TOOL_TOKENS changes the threshold.

It runs on Bash, Read, Grep, WebFetch and MCP tools, and adds about 30 ms per call.

FAQ

Does it send my code or prompts anywhere?

No. limitcast reads the limit readings Claude Code passes to your status line and the logs Claude Code and Codex already keep on your disk, and none of that leaves your machine. The only network calls are for a Pro license key: once to activate, a weekly re-check, and when you deactivate. They send your license key and computer name to Polar, our payment provider. The status line and hooks never wait on the network, and Pro keeps working offline for 30 days after the last check. If you turn on phone or webhook notifications (Pro, off by default), each notification's text goes to the service you chose (ntfy.sh or your webhook), and nothing else does.

Does it use my Claude login?

No. It never touches your subscription credentials, so it can't put your account at risk.

I use extra usage past my limit. Will the guard block me?

No. Once a window is used up, limitcast steps aside and leaves it to Claude Code. The guard only acts while you're heading toward a limit.

Does it work with Codex and Grok?

Codex: yes, forecasts, the subagent guard and the burn report, from the logs Codex already writes. Grok: yes, the burn report, a status line, the trim advisor and the subagent guard. Grok keeps no percentage used on disk, only spend, so the status line shows your 5-hour and 7-day spend; set a dollar cap (limitcast budget grok 20, Pro) and limitcast forecasts that window and the guard blocks new Grok subagents when it is on pace to run out.

Is the dollar figure what I actually pay?

No. The dollar figure is what the same tokens would cost at the API's list prices. A Claude subscription is a flat monthly fee, so the figure is a yardstick for how much your plan covers, not a bill. limitcast prices Claude models only: tokens on other models are left out and counted separately, never as zero. The prices are in the code as of 26 October 2026, and you can change them in a small file.

What if I have two Claude accounts?

Sessions of the same account share one history (matched by their limit reset times and usage that only rises), so even a short session gets a forecast, and two accounts used in parallel never mix.

Will one heavy hour make it say I'll run out of my weekly limit?

No. The 5-hour window is forecast from the last hour's pace. The weekly window uses your average pace over the last full day, idle time included, so a burst on Monday doesn't predict a runout by Wednesday. The exception is a burst that would use up the week within the next hour: that still triggers the warning and the subagent guard.

How does the free trial work?

Pick yearly or monthly and you get Pro for 14 days at no charge: your license key is ready as soon as you check out, in Polar's customer portal (linked from your confirmation email). Your card is charged when the trial ends. Cancel before then, in the same portal, and you pay nothing and the key stops working.

Can I get a refund?

Yes, within 14 days of any charge, no questions asked. Email support@limitcast.dev from the address you bought with and we'll refund it and cancel the subscription.
